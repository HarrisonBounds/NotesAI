**Lecture 1: Introduction to Greek Drama**

**Who is the instructor?**
Professor Peter Meineck, Clinical Assistant Professor of Classics, New York University

**Why study Greek drama?**

* Study of Greek drama is important because it has had a profound influence on Western drama
* Greek drama has influenced later playwrights, including Shakespeare
* It has had an impact on art forms such as opera, film, and dance
* It continues to be performed and studied today

**What is Greek drama?**
* Greek drama refers to the plays written by ancient Greek playwrights
* It includes tragedy, comedy, and satire
* Greek drama was performed in ancient Greece, particularly in Athens in the 5th century BCE

**Understanding Greek drama**

* It is important to understand the cultural and historical context in which the plays were written and performed
* The plays were written for a specific audience and were meant to be performed
* The plays used a combination of text, music, dance, and visual elements to convey meaning

**Key concepts**

* Theater as a visual art form (theatron means "seeing place")
* The chorus as a group of actors who sang and danced
* Masks as a key element of performance
* The use of language to convey complex ideas and themes
* The importance of understanding the historical and cultural context of the plays

**Tools for studying Greek drama**

* Understanding the life and times of the playwrights
* Knowledge of Greek history and culture
* Familiarity with the geography of Greece
* Understanding of the typical day at the Greek theater in Athens in the mid-5th century BCE

**Approach to studying Greek drama**

* The instructor's approach is to combine classical scholarship with practical experience in the theater
* He emphasizes the importance of understanding the plays as living art forms, rather than just as textual artifacts
* He encourages students to think about the plays as a form of popular entertainment, rather than just as academic subjects.
